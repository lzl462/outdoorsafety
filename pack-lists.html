Pack lists

These lists come from the project’s existing checklists. They are not official gear lists.

Hiking Biking Climbing Winter

Hiking

The 10 essentials

  • Navigation: map, compass or GPS, and the skill to use them.
  • Headlamp and spare batteries.
  • Sun protection.
  • Extra food and at least 1 litre of water per person.
  • Extra warm layer and rain shell.
  • First aid kit, knife or multi-tool, fire starter, emergency shelter, whistle.

Sea-to-Sky extras

  • Trail shoes or boots with grip — not fashion sneakers.
  • Microspikes if snow or ice is possible (Chief, Gondola trails, Joffre, Garibaldi).
  • Bear spray where legal, and knowledge of how to use it.
  • Do not start long alpine trips after late morning.

Also from the hiking trip plan

  • Pack food and garbage away.
  • A map that works with no service.
  • Phone battery saved for a real call.
  • Far from cell towers, a satellite messenger.

Biking

  • A layer you can put on
  • A jacket that blocks rain
  • Extra warm clothes
  • Extra food
  • Extra water
  • Food and garbage packed away
  • A headlamp
  • A whistle
  • A map that works with no service
  • Phone battery saved for a real call
  • Slow down where the view is blocked
  • Keep a dog beside you

Climbing

This list does not include ropes or a helmet.

  • A layer you can put on
  • A jacket that blocks rain
  • Extra warm clothes
  • Extra food
  • Extra water
  • Food and garbage packed away
  • A headlamp
  • A whistle
  • Shoes that match snow or ice, or you head back down
  • A map that works with no service
  • Phone battery saved for a real call
  • On a slope that can slide, go only if the group has practiced and has the tools

Winter

Check before you go

  • Read the Avalanche Canada bulletin: avalanche.ca
  • Mountain weather, not only the town forecast.
  • DriveBC for Highway 99. Stay off unmaintained forest service roads.
  • Resort boundary is not a safety boundary. Out-of-bounds is backcountry.

Every person in the group

  • Transceiver worn on the body, tested that morning.
  • Shovel and probe.
  • Partner who can search. Do not travel alone in avalanche terrain.
  • AST 1 (or equivalent) training at minimum.

This is not an official rescue site. In an emergency, call 9-1-1.
