Tips

Click a box to open it.

Be prepared.

Not being prepared for an emergency is a common mistake. Bring a layer you can put on, a jacket that blocks rain, extra warm clothes, extra food, extra water, a headlamp, and a whistle. Pack food and garbage away.

Leave your trip plan with someone before you start.

Fill in the time, the date, your starting point, your destination, your route, the people with you, and your contact numbers. Write when they should call 9-1-1 if you are not back. Leave that page with someone you trust.

Turn around at the time you set, even if the top is close.

Set a clock time to turn around, and turn around then. People often need help because they are still out when the light is gone.

Call 9-1-1 early.

If you are lost, or someone is hurt, call 9-1-1 right away. Help can take a long time.

Talk while you walk, especially on a bend and beside a creek.

Bears and cougars use these trails. A bend you cannot see around, and the noise of a creek, hide you. Talking lets the animal hear you and move off. Leave them space. A normal voice is enough.

If a creek is higher on the way back, do not cross.

A creek that was easy in the morning can be too high on the way back. Fast water, or water that is climbing, means you do not cross.

Save your phone battery for a real call.

Plenty of valleys and ridges have no signal. Save the battery for a real call. If a call goes through, it is 9-1-1. Bring a separate light, and download a map that works with no service before you leave.

This is not an official rescue site. In an emergency, call 9-1-1.
