Tips

Open a reminder for a little more detail and a practical next step.

Call 9-1-1 early if you are lost or someone is hurt

Call 9-1-1. Tell the call taker where you are and what has happened. Stay on the line, answer their questions, and follow their instructions.

Immediate actions and waiting for help

Pack for the wait, not only for the walk.

Bring the shared essentials and extra warmth for a stop or delay. Know how to use your equipment. Check the shared essentials.

Leave your plan with someone who knows when to act.

Agree on the route, return or check-in time, and when they should call 9-1-1 if you do not return. Do this for familiar trips too. Plan with your contact.

Turn around at the time you set, even if the top is close.

Watch daylight, progress, and the group’s energy. The return journey still takes time. Unease or pressure within the group is a reason to stop and reconsider. Check the turnaround advice.

Check the route behind the photo.

Look at distance, elevation, terrain, and reliable local information. One person’s easy trip may be too hard for someone else. See common assumptions and better choices.

Talk while you walk, especially on a bend and beside a creek.

Bears and cougars use these trails. Talking lets animals hear you. Leave them space and pack food and garbage away. Read the local risk advice.

If a creek is higher on the way back, do not cross.

Fast or rising water means you do not cross. Reconsider the route and call for help if you cannot return safely. Know what to do if something goes wrong.

Plan for no cell service.

Download maps before leaving, save battery for emergency contact, and plan a suitable communication backup. Knowing your GPS position does not itself send an SOS. Plan for a missing signal.

This is not an official rescue site. In an emergency, call 9-1-1.
