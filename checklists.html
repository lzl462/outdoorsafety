Before you leave

A good trip starts with a realistic plan, the skills to follow it, and equipment you know how to use.

Make and leave your trip plan

This is a printable sheet to fill in by hand. Print it, write the route, people, turnaround and return times, then give a copy to someone reliable. Agree when they should call 9-1-1 if you have not returned or checked in.

Open the printable trip plan

Pack for more than the planned trip

Bring the shared essentials as well as what your activity and the conditions need. Prepare to stay warm if you have to stop or wait for help.

Open the essentials and activity notes

The three T’s

  • Trip Planning: check the route, elevation gain, mountain weather, terrain, water, access, and the return journey.
  • Training: choose a trip that matches everyone’s fitness and experience. Know how to navigate, use your equipment, and respond if something goes wrong.
  • Taking the Essentials: bring the shared emergency basics, then add what your particular trip needs.

AdventureSmart explains the three T’s and offers the Survive Outside program.

Check the conditions for your actual route

  • Route and closures: check the land manager or route operator’s current trail information. For provincial parks, start with BC Parks’ park finder and active advisories.
  • Weather: check Environment and Climate Change Canada for your destination and warnings. Consider elevation and exposed terrain, not only the weather in town.
  • Road access: check DriveBC for highway conditions and confirm the final access road with the route operator or land manager.
  • Avalanche terrain: check the current Avalanche Canada forecast and choose terrain within your training.

Check again before leaving. These links lead to current information; this site does not reproduce live forecasts or closure notices.

Four checks before you go

  1. Can everyone in your group manage the distance, elevation gain, terrain, and return journey?
  2. When will you turn around, and what changes would make you turn around sooner?
  3. Who has your plan, and when have you agreed they should call 9-1-1 if you have not returned or checked in?
  4. What will keep you warm and help you call for help if the trip takes longer than expected?

Give your contact a job, not just a destination

Leave the plan with someone reliable, including on a short or familiar trip. Make sure they know the route and the return or check-in time you agreed on. If you miss that arrangement, they should call 9-1-1 and share your plan.

Check in when you return. Think about communication without cell service before you leave.

This is not an official rescue site. In an emergency, call 9-1-1.
