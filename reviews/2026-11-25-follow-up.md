## R01 — Visible keyboard focus

Two independently observed issues can be addressed in one bounded styling pass:

- At 1440 × 1000, the contents-list links fill the 208-pixel scroll container. Their outward focus outline is clipped at the sides by overflow:auto. Horizontal parts remain visible; this is not wholly invisible focus or a keyboard-navigation blocker. Evidence: [focus rules](../styles.css#L82), [contents container](../styles.css#L740), [captured screenshot](assets/focus-outline-desktop-2026-11-25.png). A second reviewer reproduced it independently.
- The global red focus outline (#9c3827) against the pine footer (#173c32) has a calculated contrast ratio of 1.74:1. A lighter indicator on dark surfaces would be clearer. Evidence: [focus rules](../styles.css#L82), [footer styling](../styles.css#L1222). The coordinator independently recalculated the ratio. [W3C's non-text contrast guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html) supports a 3:1 adjacent-contrast target for an author-defined focus indicator.

Possible direction: keep the contents list's useful vertical scrolling, but provide space or an inset indicator locally; use a contrasting focus treatment on dark sections. Do not remove all overflow handling merely to hide the symptom.

Acceptance: Tab through contents links, print controls, the dark Home section and footer at normal and short desktop heights. The complete indicator stays visible and has adequate adjacent contrast. Recheck mobile focus and the existing no-JS menu behavior.

## R02 — A clear calling trigger for the trusted contact

The [contact paragraph](../checklists.html#L291) says “If you miss that arrangement” after discussing a return/check-in time. The printable plan separately records the deadline for calling 9-1-1. Making that agreed calling time explicit would reduce interpretation for a beginner.

Acceptance: the paragraph, four planning checks, reminder summary and printable sheet agree about the contact's calling trigger. Preserve sharing the plan. Do not introduce a universal waiting period, extra agency selection or a different emergency number.

Acceptance: the existing number stays unchanged and readable on a phone. Explain reporting without presenting it as a general wildlife-advice or rescue line. The simple emergency 9-1-1 instruction remains dominant; no additional numbers or agency-selection flow are needed.

## R05 — Smooth the phone/call sequence in the hiking example

The [opening story paragraph](../about.html#L174) puts phone-battery failure before a later successful 9-1-1 call. Both events are reported, but the compressed sequence can read like a contradiction. This is an editorial ambiguity, not evidence that the current account is false.

Possible direction: use the declining-battery description from [the 7 September CityNews account](https://vancouver.citynews.ca/2026/09/07/squamish-bc-missing-hiker-found-shares-story/) while preserving the call/outcome supported by [4 September reporting](https://vancouver.citynews.ca/2026/09/04/squamish-hiker-found-alive/). Check the sources before introducing temporal words or causal explanations.

Acceptance: a fresh reader follows the sequence without an invented mechanism. Keep the stay-put lesson separate from the reported outcome. Avoid battery-revival mechanics, extra survival techniques or a longer incident reconstruction.

## R06 — An optional compact mobile contents list

The [Before you leave contents list](../checklists.html#L139) repeats several long headings. Both navigation reviewers measured a block around 292 pixels high. Their first-advice positions differed by about 20 pixels, so treat those coordinates as illustrative rather than a precise acceptance threshold.

Shorter navigation labels might help people reading or jumping through the guide reach its substance sooner. The prominent Printable trip plan button is already visible without scrolling at the tested 320 × 568 and 390 × 844 sizes. The primary printing journey is not blocked.

Acceptance: keep the full section headings, existing anchors and 44-pixel targets. Compare actual reading and section-jump tasks at both widths. Adopt shorter labels only if their meaning remains clear; fewer pixels alone do not prove better usability.
